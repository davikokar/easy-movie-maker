Notes

  1. 1App media folder. Imported media is copied into app storage and shared between projects. This card shows its size and removes media no project uses. Open package imports a .mmpack through the system file picker.
  2. 2Recovery is a banner, not a modal, so the app opens straight away.
  3. 3Time-scaled strip with a fixed playhead. Scrolling the strip scrubs. Captions, narration, and music sit under the pictures on the same time scale, as thin read-only lanes.
  4. 4Selection. Tap selects a clip and shows handles. Dragging a handle changes a photo's duration or trims a video. Long-press lifts the clip so it can be dragged to a new position.
  5. 5Transitions. The hatched overlap is tappable and opens the transition sheet for the incoming clip.
  6. 6Sheets keep the preview visible. Tapping a preset plays the transition in the preview at once.
  7. 7Duration limits follow the minimum-duration rule, and the sheet shows the maximum.
  8. 8Trim screen. A full-screen editor for a video clip: trim handles on a filmstrip, speed, pitch, clip audio, audio fades, and stabilization.
  9. 9Split cuts at the playhead shown in the preview.
  10. 10Audio view. A larger, editable version of the lanes. Pictures are shown for orientation. The selected narration clip shows its anchor link.
  11. 11Record narration at the playhead. The new clip is anchored to the clip shown there.
  12. 12Ducking is one switch. The orange line shows its effect.

Open questions

  • Mobile strip: time-scaled (shown), or fixed-size cards like the desktop storyboard?
  • Narration recording: tap to start and stop (shown), or press and hold?
  • Should picture playback continue during narration recording, so the speaker can follow the pictures?
  • Captions: edit text on the preview, or in a text sheet?
  • Android tablets and landscape: reuse the phone layout, or use a layout closer to the desktop?
  • Should "Clean up unused media" ask for confirmation, listing the files to delete?
